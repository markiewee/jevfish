# Pureloft: what should a 4-bedroom at Suasana Bukit Ceylon cost per night?

Real operating scenario. Every fact about the property and its pricing is taken from
Pureloft's own records. No guest data appears anywhere in this document.

## The property

The Pureloft Sdn. Bhd. operates short-stay units inside **Suasana Suites Bukit Ceylon**, a
serviced-residence tower in central Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, a short walk from the Bukit
Bintang shopping and nightlife district and the KLCC area. Ten of its units in this building
are the same configuration: **4 bedrooms, 3 bathrooms, sleeping up to 10 guests**. They are
full apartments with a kitchen, a living room, a washing machine and building facilities
including a pool.

They are sold as nightly short-stay accommodation on **Airbnb, Booking.com, and directly
over WhatsApp**. Bookings are managed through a channel manager. There is no front desk,
no restaurant and no daily housekeeping. Guests get a door code and let themselves in.

## The dates

**June and July 2026.** This is the Malaysian and Singaporean mid-year school-holiday period,
when families travel, and Kuala Lumpur draws heavily from Singapore, Indonesia and the Gulf.
Nothing unusual is scheduled, no single large event dominates the period.

## The decision

Pureloft must set one nightly rate for these units across all channels. Five options:

- **MYR 300 a night.** This is the current rate. At full capacity that is 30 ringgit a head.
- **MYR 200 a night.**
- **MYR 250 a night.**
- **MYR 400 a night.**
- **MYR 500 a night.**

Nothing else changes between the options. Same apartment, same building, same dates, same
platforms, same self check-in, same cancellation terms. Only the nightly rate differs.

## What a guest is choosing between

A traveller looking at these dates in central Kuala Lumpur can book:

- **Other hosts in the same building.** Suasana Bukit Ceylon has many individually owned
  units listed by different operators, so several near-identical apartments appear in the
  same search, at prices each host sets independently.
- **Hotel rooms in Bukit Bintang and KLCC.** Kuala Lumpur has a deep and competitive hotel
  market running from budget chains to international luxury. A hotel room sleeps two or
  three, so a family of eight or ten needs three or four rooms, and pays per room.
- **Other serviced apartments** across the city centre, many of them newer buildings.
- **Staying somewhere cheaper further out** and taking Grab or the monorail in.

The group size is what makes this unusual. A party of eight to ten choosing between one
apartment and four hotel rooms is not comparing like with like, and the per-person
arithmetic is very different from the per-room arithmetic.

## Who is actually booking these dates

- **Extended families and multi-generational groups**, often Singaporean or Malaysian,
  driving up or flying in for the school holidays. They want everyone under one roof, a
  kitchen, and somewhere the children can be noisy. Price matters, but the alternative is
  several hotel rooms, so they tend to compare total trip cost rather than nightly rate.
- **Groups of friends travelling together**, typically younger, splitting the cost. They are
  the most price-aware per head and will move for a cheaper listing.
- **Gulf family travellers.** Kuala Lumpur is an established summer destination for them.
  They travel in large groups, stay longer, and are markedly less price-sensitive.
- **Indonesian shopping-trip visitors**, short stays, weekend-heavy, focused on proximity to
  Bukit Bintang.
- **Work groups and crews** needing several beds in one place, booked by whoever is
  organising, sometimes on company money.
- **Couples and solo travellers** who will occasionally take a large apartment if the price
  falls close to a hotel room, but for whom four bedrooms is mostly wasted space.

## The question

At each of the five nightly rates, what proportion of the people in this market who are
looking at central Kuala Lumpur for these dates would book this apartment?
